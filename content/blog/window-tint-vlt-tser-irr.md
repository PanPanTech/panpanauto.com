# How to Compare Automotive Window Tint Specs: VLT, TSER and IRR

Start with the visible light transmittance (VLT) needed on the vehicle's glass. Compare total solar energy rejection (TSER) for films that reach a similar installed VLT on the same reference glass. Use infrared rejection (IRR) as a supporting figure after checking the wavelength band and whether the test used film alone or film on glass. This sequence helps an installer or distributor compare two spec sheets without buying the wrong shade or overselling the effect on cabin heat.

Look for a row tied to the exact series and shade. A brochure can display the highest result from several shades even when the roll you plan to stock has a different performance profile. Start your comparison with the row that corresponds to the material you would actually order.

## Read the numbers in the order you make the purchase

Each metric answers a different question. VLT describes the light that reaches the eye, measured using a standard weighting across the visible spectrum. TSER describes how much incident solar energy the tested glazing system keeps from entering as heat under stated conditions. IRR describes the rejection of infrared radiation within the specified spectral range. Ask the supplier what each percentage is based on before transferring it to a price list or customer quote. [3M automotive window film comparison guide](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/2576673O/personal-auto-awf-product-comparison-guide-my.pdf)

| Metric | Decision it supports | Detail to request alongside the number |
|---|---|---|
| VLT | Choose a suitable visible-light level for the intended glazing and application. | Film-only or combined result, reference glass and exact shade. |
| TSER | Compare broad solar heat control among candidates at similar installed VLT. | Glass type, thickness and baseline VLT, test or calculation method, exact shade. |
| IRR | Understand a claim about a specified segment of the infrared spectrum. | Wavelength or band, instrument or method, sample configuration and liner condition. |
| IRER | Compare a defined infrared energy metric where suppliers report an equivalent measure. | Wavelength band and whether the value accounts for an inward share of absorbed infrared energy. |

A useful quote names the series, shade and roll width beside the data. The product family name can stay on the price list, but keep the identifying rows and measurement notes with purchasing records so the salesperson and technician can find the same specification later.

## VLT depends on both the film and the original glass

Higher VLT means more visible light passes through the material or assembly being described. A shade name printed on a carton can refer to a nominal value. A published figure for film applied to a reference pane already includes the effect of that pane. Ask which basis the supplier uses and whether every row in the table uses the same reference glass. A technical sheet can report different values for the same film on clear and tinted glass. [3M Ceramic IR technical data sheet](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1919598O/3m-automotive-window-film-ceramic-ir-series-tech-data-sheet.pdf)

When you have a true film-only value and a reading for the original vehicle pane, multiplying the two decimal fractions can help you screen a potential combination. In a hypothetical example, glass at 80% VLT and a film at 70% film-only VLT give a rough estimate of 0.80 × 0.70 = 0.56, or 56% combined VLT. The actual result depends on the spectral properties of both materials and their interaction. A published combined value belongs with the glass used to obtain it, so ask for film-only data or a directly comparable combined measurement if you need to screen a different pane.

Measure each relevant pane using equipment and a method appropriate to the job and market, then record the vehicle, window position and meter reading. Original glass can differ between front and rear positions. Check local requirements for the particular vehicle and window before taking a booking with a stated VLT. Where the installer needs confirmation, verify the completed installation using the applicable measurement procedure. [International Window Film Association automotive guidance](https://iwfa.com/automotive/)

## TSER compares overall solar energy under stated conditions

Incoming solar energy includes ultraviolet, visible and infrared radiation. Some passes through the glazing, some is reflected and some is absorbed. A portion of what the glazing absorbs can flow to the interior as heat. That inward portion matters when comparing broad solar heat gain. Treat TSER as a measure of the fraction of total incident solar energy kept outside the tested system, and use the supplier's definition and method to confirm how the inward contribution is handled. [U.S. Department of Energy definition of solar heat gain coefficient](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2013/12/f5/doe_building_energy_data_exchange_specification_02-15-13.pdf) [3M automotive window film comparison guide](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/2576673O/personal-auto-awf-product-comparison-guide-my.pdf)

Compare TSER values at similar installed VLT first, using equivalent glass and methods. A darker shade may get a larger TSER figure partly by reducing visible transmission. If the customer wants to keep the front cabin bright, the useful comparison is among films that preserve a similar amount of visible light. A heat-control claim should also identify whether it describes the whole film-and-glass system or an improvement relative to the same bare reference glass. If an improvement is needed, ask for the baseline and the filmed result from the same method.

For example, under matching reference conditions, a move from 40% TSER to 50% TSER is a 10-percentage-point increase in rejection. On a scale of 100 units of incident solar energy, the admitted share goes from 60 units to 50 in that comparison. That calculation describes the glazing numbers in the example. Cabin temperature also depends on the amount and orientation of glazing, how long the vehicle sits in the sun, other surfaces, ventilation and air-conditioning. Offer a matching-condition product comparison a customer can understand and keep the number tied to the window system tested.

## Inspect the wavelengths behind IRR and IRER

The label IRR sometimes appears without a band or a description of the sample. A reading at one wavelength and an energy-based result integrated across a broad band both concern infrared radiation, yet they describe different measurements. An official automotive film technical sheet provides a useful example of the distinction. It reports infrared rejection over 900 to 1,000 nanometers for film with its liner and an infrared energy rejection value over 780 to 2,500 nanometers for film applied to glass. The latter also accounts for the inward effect of some absorbed energy. These are the specific definitions on that sheet. Ask each supplier to define the figures it quotes. [3M Ceramic IR technical data sheet](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/1919598O/3m-automotive-window-film-ceramic-ir-series-tech-data-sheet.pdf)

An infrared lamp or a handheld meter can help in a controlled sales demonstration. Record which instrument you used and what it measures. A display driven by a narrow-band source can emphasize that source's wavelength. Keep the demonstration under repeatable conditions and attach the full technical table to any comparison that will guide a purchase. If a supplier gives you a high IRR and no method, ask for the underlying report or treat IRR as unresolved in the scorecard.

For a distributor, the same rule applies to catalog filters. Keep measured TSER and IRR in separate fields and store the infrared band next to the value. That lets branches answer a customer who has seen a larger number on a different brochure and makes it easier to correct the catalog when the supplier revises its method or replaces a shade.

## Compare two offers with an evidence-first worksheet

Consider two hypothetical candidates for the same installation package. The customer wants a light appearance and asks which film better reduces solar heat. These example numbers show how to handle incomplete data before ordering.

| Field | Candidate A | Candidate B | Buyer decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| VLT on the published reference pane | 55% on clear reference glass | 54% on tinted reference glass | Obtain results on matching glass or test representative samples. |
| TSER | 47% on the specified clear pane | 52% on the specified tinted pane | Keep the figures on file. Request comparable conditions before ranking. |
| IRR | 90%, 900 to 1,000 nm, film with liner | 95%, method omitted | Ask for Candidate B's band and sample setup. |
| Fit for the stock plan | Desired width quoted | Width and dispatch date pending | Confirm that the usable shade and width will arrive before the package launches. |

The data suggest two avenues for follow-up. First, ask the suppliers to put the competing shades on the same reference glass and disclose the test or calculation method. If one can provide only a different glass configuration, keep that document for product reference and hold the numerical ranking. Second, use identifiable samples to assess how each would look on the shop's representative glazing. Record initial and applied readings together, then decide whether the finish, visible light level and documented broad solar control meet the needs of that package.

Use a similar sample for each branch or dealer display. Label the pane, film series and shade so a customer cannot mistake one film for another. Note visual considerations that matter to the package, such as color cast and reflections viewed from the driver's position. Evaluate these features separately from the solar comparison. Keep stock decisions tied to the color, grade and dimensions available from the supplier.

## Get the missing data before buying a mixed-shade order

Ask for a technical data sheet that identifies each shade you plan to order. Request the reference glass type, thickness and baseline VLT used for the combined data. Ask whether each result is tested or calculated and which method the manufacturer used. For infrared claims, request the wavelength range, whether the specimen included the liner and whether the reported value describes the film or the composite. Keep the issue date or revision alongside your supplier quote.

Review the paperwork with the installer responsible for application. Confirm the widths required for your common vehicles and whether each shade comes in those widths. Ask for manufacturer installation and care instructions for the specific series and request written warranty terms. Before central purchasing fills several branches, evaluate a small assortment against actual orders and leave a place to record returns or installation issues by roll and lot. Repeat the check when a supplier changes a series or sends a different spec sheet with a new shipment.

At the sales counter, give a customer three facts in plain English. State the expected visible light level for the glazing being discussed, explain the broad heat-control comparison and identify the basis of any infrared figure you show. Keep the full technical sheet available for buyers comparing multiple rolls. This approach also gives sales and installation staff the same reference when a customer asks about the result in a different window position.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which specification should I compare first?

Start with the installed VLT you need for the intended application. Among comparable shades, use TSER on equivalent reference glass for broad solar heat control. Read the IRR footnote before comparing the infrared performance claims.

### Does a higher IRR figure mean the film keeps more solar heat out overall?

A higher IRR figure represents stronger rejection within its own stated measurement basis. Compare TSER under equivalent conditions to judge broad solar performance. Ask for the test method if the two infrared figures have different wavelength ranges or sample configurations.

### Can two films with a similar VLT have different TSER?

Yes. Two films can pass similar amounts of visible light while the film-and-glass systems handle solar energy differently. Compare the exact shades on a matching reference assembly and verify that both figures describe the same type of result.

### Can I use the printed shade name as the installed VLT?

Use it to identify the product row, then read the corresponding data and the stated reference glass. Check the vehicle pane and verify the installed result using the applicable measurement procedure when the job calls for that confirmation.

### Does a ceramic label make the comparison easier?

The label tells you how the supplier describes the series. Use the exact shade's documented VLT and TSER, the infrared test conditions and a representative sample to evaluate it. Keep the shade and test basis in the purchase record in case the series changes.
