# Clear Gloss vs. Matte PPF: What Should Your Shop Stock First?

Stock clear gloss paint protection film first if most of your customers want to preserve glossy paint and book partial-front or full-front packages. Keep a matte sample ready for consultations and buy matte against confirmed jobs while demand takes shape. If factory matte vehicles or full-body satin conversions already occupy a predictable share of your calendar, reserve matte stock for those bookings. Let recent paid jobs, compatible roll widths and supplier replenishment times set the size of your first order.

The inventory risk comes from two directions. A missing roll can delay a booked installation. An extra roll in a slow finish ties up money and may still be the wrong width for the next job. A small assortment can work when each roll has a defined purpose and the shop knows when to reorder it.

## Start with the finish the customer wants to see

Clear gloss PPF is a straightforward choice for a customer who likes the look of their glossy paint and wants protection on selected panels. Ask them to look at a sample across a panel edge in daylight. They may notice differences in texture, reflections or the visibility of a seam even when the film and paint are both glossy. A sample on paint tells them more than a roll of film on its liner.

Matte or satin PPF softens the appearance of reflections. Shops typically discuss it for two distinct jobs. The first is protecting an existing low-gloss finish. The second is changing glossy paint to a satin or matte appearance. Even when two films carry the word *matte*, their sheen can differ. Compare the sample against the actual vehicle, particularly if any adjacent panels will stay uncovered. Manufacturer literature illustrates how gloss and satin films can change a vehicle's appearance. [XPEL paint protection film FAQs](https://www.xpel.com/en-au/faqs)

Evaluate protective claims and warranty terms for the exact SKU. Ask how the supplier measures any claimed performance and what its warranty covers on the intended surface. Published manufacturer warranties illustrate the importance of checking exclusions, installation requirements and the process for submitting a claim. [XPEL warranty information](https://www.xpel.com/warranty-information)

## Choose the package before choosing the roll

A customer may ask for the same finish on two vehicles and require very different film allocations. A hood with a large pattern can dictate the roll width for a full-front package. A full-body satin conversion adds doors, quarters and other coverage that may require a second roll or a different size mix. Check the actual panel list and cut plan before giving the customer a firm installation date.

| Job in the calendar | First finish to evaluate | Check before reserving material |
|---|---|---|
| Glossy daily driver with a full-front booking | Clear gloss | Plot hood and bumper patterns at the intended roll width. Show the customer where any coverage line sits. |
| Glossy car with isolated impact areas | Clear gloss | Compare the look of a covered panel and its uncovered neighbor before approving partial coverage. |
| Factory matte car with a front protection request | Matte or satin | Match a sample to the actual paint in diffuse and directional light. Let the customer approve any visible boundary. |
| Glossy car booked for a full-body satin conversion | Matte or satin | Agree which painted surfaces, trim-adjacent areas and returns are included. Reserve enough of the approved film for the whole job and a likely recut. |
| Repeat work for a dealer with a known vehicle mix | Use the finish agreed for each package | Tie each package to a measured cutting plan and replenishment schedule. |

For a finish conversion, walk the vehicle with the customer and mark the edges of the scope. Door openings, mirror bases and areas around emblems are easy to overlook on a quick quote. Decide together how much of the original finish will remain visible. Document the agreed scope with photos or a simple panel list, then reserve material against that list.

## Use recent bookings to size the first order

Take the last eight to twelve weeks of quotes and completed work. For each record, note the paint finish, requested look, quoted package, booked package, film SKU, roll width and usable length consumed. Count the cuts that could be reused separately from scrap. Include installation jobs your shop declined or postponed because the correct film was unavailable. For a new shop with little history, start with deposits, signed dealer orders and a few recent conversations in which customers accepted a specific package and price.

Separate the evidence by strength. A scheduled vehicle with a confirmed finish gives you a stronger basis for reserving material than a customer who asked about satin over the phone. Record sample requests as evidence of interest. When those consultations begin yielding paid bookings, use the conversion history to decide whether to keep a matte roll on the shelf. A distributor can run the same exercise by account and check how much demand depends on its largest customer.

Consider an illustrative shop that completes ten full-front jobs and two full-body conversions during a review period. All ten full-front jobs consume clear gloss from one compatible width. The two satin jobs require larger allocations and arrive on irregular dates. The shop has two different stocking decisions. For clear gloss, the owner can track typical weekly consumption and reorder before the next shipment would leave the cutting table empty. For satin, the owner can order against the booking and allocate one finish and lot where available. A third conversation about a satin finish is a useful signal for the showroom, even while it remains outside the material reserved for production.

## Calculate coverage and a practical reorder point

Start with the pattern library or the measured dimensions your team uses. Lay out the proposed pieces at the chosen width and estimate how much length the cut plan takes, including allowed margins. A roll with more square footage can still fail to yield a hood in one planned piece if it lacks the needed width. Measure leftovers in width and uninterrupted length and record which jobs can actually use those pieces.

Use three numbers in the stock log. **Usable on-hand length** is what your team can cut from the relevant width and finish. **Reserved length** belongs to booked vehicles. **Free length**, the difference between the two, is available for new work. Keep short remnants out of the main balance if they only fit small pieces. Review free length against the work you expect before the next confirmed replenishment date.

Suppose one clear gloss width has 18 usable meters on hand and 10 meters reserved for booked work. Free length is 8 meters. If your history suggests another 6 meters of likely work before replenishment arrives and the lead installer wants 3 meters available for a likely recut, the reorder threshold on a free-length basis is 9 meters. At 8 meters, check the next confirmed dispatch date and place the replenishment order if timing still calls for it. The numbers illustrate the calculation. Set the allowance from actual recuts, compatible patterns and the consequences of delaying a booking.

For a slow-moving finish, use the same logic against each booked job. Confirm the supplier's current ship date, check the panel fit and name the employee responsible for placing the order. When you set the appointment, allow time to receive and inspect the material. If a regular customer needs several identical vehicles, discuss reserve quantities and a recurring delivery cadence before buying the whole run.

## Compare samples under the same conditions

Prepare a glossy panel in a color the shop sees often and, when the work calls for it, use a suitable factory matte test panel with the owner's permission. Apply candidate films to comparable sections following their respective manufacturer instructions. Keep an uncovered section for comparison. Label each sample with the series, SKU and lot. Give the samples the same lighting, viewing distance and inspection time after installation. Manufacturers may publish application and care instructions for specific film series. [3M paint protection film installation guidelines](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/2159838O/3m-paint-protection-film-installation-guidelines-en-we.pdf)

Ask the lead installer to evaluate placement, the result around a representative contour and any edge or seam that customers are likely to see. Ask the salesperson to record which appearance the customer prefers and why. Look at the same panel near a window and under shop lights. Photograph from repeated angles with the same lighting and camera settings where practical, since exposure or reflections can make a side-by-side look more dramatic than it does in person.

Keep the sample board in the quote area with the current SKU clearly marked. When a supplier changes a series or you receive a different lot for a visually sensitive job, check the new material against the approved sample before cutting. Include the correct film care sheet at handoff, especially for a satin finish the customer has never maintained. [XPEL product care instructions](https://www.xpel.com/en-au/product-care)

## Ask the supplier for a quote you can stock against

Give the supplier your main packages and the widths your team wants to trial. Ask for a separate line for each finish and width, along with the exact series, nominal roll length and minimum order requirement. Clarify whether a trial quantity is available and how the supplier handles mixed finishes in an order. Confirm which items can ship from current stock, which require a later dispatch and how a short shipment would affect the rest of the order.

Ask for a technical sheet that identifies the SKU. Check whether its thickness measurement includes the adhesive or release liner and whether related gloss and matte items have separate data sheets. Request written storage guidance and the handling instructions that apply to the material as delivered. Discuss packaging and roll labels so your staff can match the shipment to the purchase order and capture the lot before opening each roll.

Bring finance into the choice when slow stock would force you to give up more frequently used widths. A matte roll may earn a place in the budget once confirmed bookings consume it within your normal reorder cycle. Evaluate the cost of keeping one available alongside the cost of an urgent delivery or a rescheduled job. Include the usable length and expected pattern yield when you compare quotes for different roll sizes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should a new shop keep both finishes in full rolls?

Start with the materials you can allocate to specific work. If you expect several glossy full-front jobs, buy the clear gloss width those patterns require. Keep an identified matte sample for consultations and arrange a trial or job-based order. Revisit the choice once your bookings show how often you need matte and which width serves those jobs.

### Can I use clear gloss PPF on factory matte paint?

A glossy film can change the look of a matte surface. Place a sample where the customer can compare it with the uncovered paint and ask the film maker about suitability for the vehicle's finish. If the job calls for retaining the low-gloss appearance, evaluate matte or satin samples against the specific paint before quoting.

### Is matte PPF harder to maintain than clear gloss?

Care depends on the film and its finish. Ask the manufacturer which cleaning and detailing products it approves, what the owner should do about contamination and when the car can be washed after installation. Hand the customer the care instructions for their selected SKU. [XPEL product care instructions](https://www.xpel.com/en-au/product-care)

### Can I switch finish or lot halfway through a job?

For visually sensitive work, allocate sufficient material from the approved finish and lot where practical. If a shortage requires an alternative, compare a sample on the relevant paint and give the customer a chance to review the appearance before approving the additional coverage. Keep the lot details with the job record for future repairs or warranty questions.

### When does a regular matte reorder make sense?

Set a reorder quantity once you can name which packages will use the roll, roughly when you expect to consume it and how long replacements take. A standing order becomes easier to justify when booked work repeatedly absorbs the same finish and width and the extra stock reduces missed or delayed appointments.
