## View 1. The hood gives you a broad inspection area

The first video shows a squeegee working across film on a hood. The broad central area gives the viewer a reference for where the work is happening before attention shifts to the surrounding geometry. For a buyer preparing a trial, ask the installer to photograph the same area after handoff and after the follow-up interval for the chosen film. Use one wide shot to orient the viewer and a closer view that shows how light moves across the surface. [Hood process clip](https://panpanauto.com/installations/process-squeegee.mp4)

A good record makes the direction of the view apparent. Photograph along the hood from the front and then across it from one side if your light setup allows. Reflected lines can help the reviewer locate a change in appearance. Use the same position and light for the corresponding follow-up photo so a reflection moving across the panel remains easy to distinguish from a change in the film. Ask for the exact location before trying to interpret an isolated close-up of a mark.

Some wet-install methods use installation fluid while the installer positions the film and a squeegee to work it against the panel. The supplier’s current instructions should guide the procedure for the film in your trial. For example, 3M’s guide specifies different installation solutions and recommendations for particular series. Use the clip to discuss the operation with your installer and get the applicable work instructions with the sample. ## View 2. A headlight and grille put several shapes close together

The second clip shows film being worked near a headlight and grille. It gives a closer look at an area with several boundaries in a small space. The gallery’s stills also include front details of a Volkswagen crossover, with views of the grille, headlight and adjacent fender. Those stills belong to their own gallery group. Together these types of views suggest what to ask for when your shop is evaluating a bumper or front-end program. [Headlight and grille process clip](https://panpanauto.com/installations/process-ford-grille.mp4) · [Volkswagen crossover photographs](https://panpanauto.com/installations/#volkswagen-crossover-heading)

Request a work-order drawing or coverage map for the proposed job. The map should identify where the film begins and ends and where an opening, trim line or exposed area is intentional. Then ask for one photo from a normal customer viewing position and a detail from the angle the installer uses to inspect the boundary. The wide shot shows how the area reads as a whole. The close-up gives the reviewer a way to match a specific edge to the coverage map.

While planning the pilot, agree on which front corner is the most representative for the shop. A team that mainly sells hood and fender coverage needs a different trial from a team that plans to offer a complex bumper as part of a regular package. Let your own job mix decide which detailed view and application geometry the sample should cover.

## View 3. The windshield-side hood edge deserves its own record

The third clip is described in the gallery as trimming film along the hood edge near the windshield. This view is useful because it isolates a location that can disappear in front and side photographs. For procurement review, ask whether the relevant termination was specified in the pattern, whether the edge is intended to be wrapped or to stop at a defined line, and how the installer checks the finished boundary. [Hood edge process clip](https://panpanauto.com/installations/process-bumper.mp4)

There are two useful edge photos. First take a wider image with the windshield or another landmark in view. Then take a close-up of the section being assessed and label its location. If the installer finds an area that needs attention, record the observation before rework, again after the approved correction and at the planned follow-up. A buyer can then tell which state each picture represents and where to look when the shop repeats the application.

Ask the installer to use the agreed method for preparing, cutting and finishing the film on the pilot job. The trimming clip establishes the operation depicted at that edge. The installer and the current instructions determine how an equivalent area should be handled on your vehicle and material.

## Build a photo record your team can use after delivery

Start with intake pictures of the chosen panel before film goes on. Record existing chips, marks and previous paintwork where known. Then add an image of the roll label and a work-order identifier to connect the material to the vehicle. Keep those identifiers together in the internal job record. Before sharing photos, ask for the appropriate permission and follow your shop’s policy for visible plates, paperwork, people and other identifying details.

Capture the film during placement as well as at handoff. Pick the areas that matter to the buyer and make their locations clear. Include a broad panel, a visually demanding transition and the job’s most relevant edge. If the customer should review a boundary at collection, note it in the handoff record. Plan a later inspection at the interval appropriate for the film and record any feedback against the original views. Confirm the film’s wash waiting period, approved care methods and edge guidance at handoff. For an example of the detail to look for, see the separate care instructions XPEL publishes for its film lines. [XPEL product care instructions](https://www.xpel.com/product-care)

If a supplier or installer gives you a photo set with several vehicles, ask them to label each record separately. Use a simple sequence across jobs. Intake, label, context, edge, handoff and follow-up are enough for many pilots. An issue photo should also name the panel, inspection date and any rework. This structure lets a buyer and installer return to the same evidence when a question comes in after delivery.

## Turn the walkthrough into a pilot purchase

Choose a job your shop regularly sells and find the two features that matter most in its approval. An example would be the broad appearance of a clear hood application and the condition of one termination near the windshield. Give the supplier the finish, panel geometry and roll width you expect to use, and ask for the product code, current instructions and sample details. If your inspection flags an issue, send a wide and a close view with the identified film and the time of the observation. Agree on the next step before expanding the order.

For a distributor, request the same evidence from each participating shop and record who performed the work and which instructions they followed. Compare results from comparable jobs first. Review handling notes, repeatability, material use and any follow-up concerns alongside the photos. When each result ties back to a part of the vehicle and an identified product, the evidence can support a practical decision about whether to stock the material more widely.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which film is installed on the vehicles in these photographs?

The public gallery leaves the brand, series and SKU unspecified for the vehicles shown. Ask for a reference that includes the film label and a matching job record when you need to evaluate a particular series.

### Are the three clips and photos from a single job?

The gallery presents the footage as examples from the bay and organizes the stills by vehicle. Use each as a separate reference. A job-specific case needs matching records that connect the process and finished views.

### Can I tell whether an edge will stay seated from a photo?

A photo shows the visible condition at the time of capture. Ask for a later photo of the same area under similar lighting, the film’s inspection interval and relevant care guidance. For a recurring issue, ask whether the shop observed it on a comparable panel or repeat application.

### What evidence should I request to compare two finishes?

Ask for both films on a known compatible substrate under matching light. Record the original paint, viewing angle and identity of each film. Include a wide view for appearance and a close-up where film meets exposed paint or another panel.

### Which records matter when buying rolls for several shops?

Request a product code, current installation and care documents, identifiable roll labels and a consistent trial record for each site. Choose a representative job, capture the same views at each location, and use the results to decide whether additional training or another sample is needed.
