# How to Request a Wholesale PPF Quote: A Buyer’s Checklist

To request a wholesale paint protection film (PPF) quote, give the supplier each film series or finish, the roll width and length you can use, the number of rolls per line, and the delivery city and postal code. Add your stock-ready date, whether you need samples, and whether you plan to arrange freight. Ask for an itemized reply with the exact SKU, minimum order quantity, currency, lead time, delivery terms, and sample conditions. A clear first request lets an installation shop check whether a supplier can meet its schedule and gives a distributor enough information to compare offers on the same basis.

## Start with the jobs the film needs to cover

Make a short list of the jobs or customer orders this purchase needs to support. A shop that mostly installs clear film on painted front ends may want a core size it already cuts efficiently. A shop adding matte work might need a small trial quantity and a sample for customer approvals. A distributor supplying several installers may need to separate immediate demand from an estimate of future reorders. Use the actual stock need to decide which products belong on the opening quote and which should be listed as optional.

Record how your team cuts film. If a commonly used pattern requires a particular width, write that width into the request along with any acceptable alternative. If the team orders pre-cut kits, say so explicitly and give the supplier the relevant vehicle and coverage details. A roll quotation and a kit quotation serve different jobs, so give them separate lines.

## Give each requested roll its own line

Use a line number so the supplier can reply against the same structure. Include the unit with every dimension, especially if some of your purchasing records use feet and others use meters. Use the supplier’s exact series name or SKU if you know it. If you only know the finish and intended use, describe them and ask the supplier to propose an item and identify it in writing.

| Quote field | What to write | What to confirm in the reply |
|---|---|---|
| Film and finish | Clear gloss, clear matte or satin, or a specified color and finish | Brand, series, full SKU, finish, and color code if applicable |
| Roll size | Width × length, with units, and any permitted alternatives | Quoted roll dimensions and whether the stock size differs |
| Quantity | Number of rolls for each line, with alternate volumes listed separately | Minimum by SKU, minimum by order, and applicable price tiers |
| Project use | Shop trial, recurring stock, or a specific job with a deadline | Stock availability and which quoted item fits the request |
| Substitutions | Which properties may change and which approvals are required | A separate alternate line with the differences identified |

Separate finish, size, and quantity even if you plan to order a mixed shipment. A figure quoted for ten rolls may depend on buying ten of one SKU. If you want six gloss rolls and four matte rolls, ask whether the same price applies to that mix and whether the series have different minimums. An alternative finish or roll size should have its own line so you can check its effect on your jobs before approving it.

## Describe the first order and the reorder separately

Ask for the price and availability of the opening order you are ready to place. If there is a plausible reorder, show that as a second scenario and identify the quantity and interval you want priced. A shop might be ready to trial two rolls and want to understand the price at ten rolls. A distributor might need two territory-specific branches of the same quote to reflect different delivery points. Keep each scenario distinct so the supplier can show what changes with volume, destination, or SKU mix.

Ask whether the quantity break is based on a line item, a box count, a combined order, or a longer purchasing commitment. If paying for a larger tier creates stock you cannot use within your normal turnover, keep the smaller tier on your comparison. Check what payment would be due for each option, since an attractive unit price can still require more cash for the first purchase.

Give the date the film needs to be available for installation or dispatch. Ask the supplier for a stock status, the earliest estimated dispatch window, and a separate transit estimate if it can provide one. If the order depends on a project date, ask when stock can be reserved and what written confirmation you will get. Use the same ready-to-use date for every supplier you invite to quote.

## Make the sample request specific

Ask what format is available for the test you plan to run. A swatch can help with appearance or presentation. A piece large enough for a controlled install lets the shop observe handling under its own conditions. If the trial has to cover a particular curve or panel size, tell the supplier the dimensions you need. Specify the film you want sampled and ask it to label each sample so it can be matched to the quoted series and finish.

Include the delivery destination for the sample, even if it is different from the stock delivery address. Request the sample charge, courier charge, estimated dispatch, and any conditions for applying a sample charge to a later order. Ask whether you can get the technical data sheet, installation guidance, care instructions, and written warranty terms for the same SKU. Record the document revision if the file provides one. When a sample and a quote describe different items, ask for a written mapping before drawing purchasing conclusions from the test.

Decide what information will allow you to proceed after the sample arrives. A shop might define a minimum usable piece size, name the installer reviewing it, and set a date for checking appearance and edge behavior. A distributor might also want photos of the retail label and carton so it can prepare inventory records. Give the supplier any evidence it can reasonably provide before you order stock.

## Ask for an itemized commercial reply

A comparable written quote should show the quoted currency, price per unit, extended quantity and line total, validity dates, payment schedule, and what is included in the total. Ask whether additional packing, freight, insurance, customs brokerage, duties or taxes, handling, and final delivery may be charged and which party is expected to arrange them. A route with a carrier pickup may require a different price from a delivery to your receiving address. If you want both options, ask for two clearly labeled totals.

For cross-border orders with an Incoterms® rule, request the three-letter rule, exact named place or port, and version. The rule helps describe delivery responsibilities and the allocation of certain costs and risks. Confirm the quoted scope with the supplier and logistics provider, and put separate payment, acceptance, and claim terms into the commercial agreement. The destination name alone often leaves open where collection or delivery is expected to happen. [International Chamber of Commerce guidance on Incoterms® 2020](https://iccwbo.org/business-solutions/incoterms-rules/incoterms-2020/).

For a new supplier relationship, ask what happens if the goods arrive short, damaged, or with the wrong label. Request the claim window, required evidence, and who handles replacements or credits. If you buy under particular packaging or labeling requirements, include them early and ask whether they carry a separate cost. State whether the receiving location needs an appointment, liftgate, or another delivery service that should be included in the quote.

Paste the following into your RFQ, complete the fields, and keep the numbered lines when you ask other suppliers for quotes. In a follow-up, identify the RFQ date and the specific line so the parties can trace revisions.

```text

Company, buyer name, and work contact —
Business type and country —
Use case or project —

Line 1 — Film series or finish, roll width × length with units, roll quantity
Line 2 — Film series or finish, roll width × length with units, roll quantity
Permitted alternatives and properties that must stay the same —
Optional price breaks or reorder quantities —

Sample — Requested SKU or finish, usable piece size, ship-to location
Technical documents needed — Data sheet, installation guide, warranty terms
Stock ship-to — City, postal code, country, and delivery requirements
Date stock is needed on site —
Preferred pickup or delivery option —

Please reply by line with exact SKU, dimensions, quantity, MOQ, stock
status, unit price, currency, and line total. Please include quote
validity, payment terms, estimated dispatch and transit, and the
named delivery point. Itemize packing, freight, and other charges
where applicable. For samples, include charge, shipping, availability,
and any credit conditions. Please identify substitutions separately.
```

Attach a simple line-item sheet if you have more than a few SKUs or need quotes for several delivery addresses. Include your preferred unit of measurement once in the header, then repeat it with each roll dimension to protect against copying errors. The same structure works for a one-SKU trial if you delete the unused lines and retain the delivery and sample fields.

## Check the quote before you discuss payment

Start with item identity. Match the full SKU, film finish, or color code and roll dimensions to your request, and flag any variation. Then check the units, quantity breaks, and line extensions. If a quantity or size has changed, ask for the written quote and total to be reissued to reduce the risk of ordering from a mix of email versions.

Read the validity period against your decision date. Ask whether film stock is reserved, what triggers packing, and when the estimated delivery window begins. Confirm itemized charges and the expected receiving point, then record who can approve a sample, substitution, or order change. The final purchase order should identify the quote version accepted and the exact products and quantities purchased.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I request a quote without a product code?

Yes. Give the finish, roll width and length, approximate quantity, and expected installation use. Ask the supplier to return a full SKU and product data sheet. Review the proposed item against your job before the quote becomes an order.

### Should I ask for the price per roll or per square meter?

Ask for the price per roll and both roll dimensions first. Area is useful for comparing comparable film, while roll width can affect pattern fit and scrap. Give the supplier your normal working width so a lower area price cannot silently substitute a roll that fits fewer jobs.

### When should I request the freight estimate?

Provide the destination in the first request if delivered cost matters to your buying decision. Ask what the estimate covers and how long it is valid. If your own freight provider is quoting, request the pickup address, package count, dimensions, and weight when available.

### Is a sample request the same as placing a wholesale order?

A sample is a separate decision step. Ask whether the sample has a charge, whether shipping is included, and whether the sample matches the exact item you are considering. Request the wholesale quote separately so the opening quantity and sample terms are easy to verify.

### How should a distributor request quotes for more than one market?

Separate the first order, reorder estimate, and each destination. Ask the supplier to confirm which quoted series, delivery options, and commercial terms apply to each market. Keep practical requirements such as carton labeling and documents on the relevant quote line or destination.
