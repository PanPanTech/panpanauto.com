# How to Compare Wholesale PPF Quotes by Landed Cost

To compare wholesale paint protection film (PPF) quotes, put both offers in one currency and bring each to the same receiving point. Start with the quoted goods total and add the buyer-paid charges needed to get the shipment there. Account for costs already included in the supplier price before adding freight, insurance, clearance, or delivery. Then divide the total by the number of comparable rolls or the amount of film your shop expects to use. A wholesale quote can have a lower roll price and still cost more to receive. The difference becomes visible once you price both offers to the same destination.

## Define the shipment and the destination first

Start with an order both suppliers can price on a common basis. Match the finish, SKU or approved equivalent, roll width and length, quantity, and timing. If the minimum order differs, show the actual order size and its total cash commitment for each offer. A quote for twelve rolls and a quote for twenty-four can still be useful, but each needs to show the stock you would actually receive and the cash you would tie up.

Pick a physical endpoint that reflects where the order enters stock. For a shop, that may be a receiving door with an appointment and no loading dock. For a distributor, it could be a warehouse that needs deliveries booked in advance. State whether buyer-paid unloading or receiving services belong in the comparison so both columns follow the same definition. Label expenses after receipt, such as warehouse storage or installation labor, separately if they are relevant to the larger buying decision.

Write down the quote versions, dates, currencies, and expiry dates. If the currencies differ, apply one documented conversion method at the same comparison date. Track any actual settlement fee separately when it belongs in your purchasing model. Check whether the supplier quote and the logistics estimate will both remain valid when you expect to place the order.

## Independent quotes can include different delivery work

Ask for the full Incoterms® rule, named point, and version when the transaction uses one. The International Chamber of Commerce publishes these rules to clarify aspects of delivery, risk, and the allocation of costs and obligations between buyer and seller. For example, a Free Carrier (FCA) quote from a named origin point and a Delivered at Place (DAP) quote to your named warehouse can cover very different parts of the trip. Confirm which point the written offer names and what each quoted charge pays for. [ICC guidance on the place of delivery and risk transfer](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/place-of-delivery-risk-transfer-global-trade-contracts/).

With DAP, the seller arranges carriage to the agreed point, where the shipment is placed at the buyer’s disposal ready for unloading. The buyer handles import clearance where applicable. With Delivered Duty Paid (DDP), the seller takes on the import-clearance obligations under the rule. If you receive a door-delivered quote, ask for the named rule and a breakdown of what is included. Confirm with the supplier and broker who will handle the import entry and how charges and records will be documented. ICC guidance also distinguishes the Incoterms® rules from the wider contract that covers payment, quality, and remedies. [ICC guidance on DAP and DDP](https://academy.iccwbo.org/incoterms/article/incoterms-2020-dap-or-ddp/).

Work line by line with the forwarder. A freight estimate may include a domestic pickup, terminal charges, the main transport leg, or delivery from the destination terminal. Each line needs an identified owner and scope. A charge that appears on a freight estimate can already be included in the supplier’s offer, especially when the supplier has arranged a portion of carriage.

## Build a landed-cost worksheet

Use a separate column for each quote. For every possible charge, record the amount, who pays it, where it appears in writing, and whether it is included, confirmed separately, estimated, or awaiting confirmation. Show included charges with a label rather than listing them a second time in the arithmetic. Request a revised written quote when the available figures are too vague to compare.

| Cost row | What to record or verify |
|---|---|
| Supplier quote | Extended price for each SKU, stated discounts, currency, and quote validity |
| Packing and origin services | Special packaging, pickup, handling, or documentation paid outside the quote |
| Main carriage | Quoted transport route, package count and dimensions, surcharges, and expiry |
| Cargo insurance | Whether coverage is arranged, who pays the premium, limits and deductible |
| Import clearance and charges | Broker’s itemized estimate for services and applicable charges outside the quote |
| Final delivery and receiving | Destination handling, local delivery, appointment, unloading, and any agreed receiving service |
| Settlement | Applicable payment or conversion charges included in your cost model |

Use the supplier’s stated quantities and the carrier’s package measurements. If the supplier provides only a per-roll price and an estimated roll weight, request carton or pallet dimensions so your forwarder can price the actual shipment. For applicable import charges, use the destination-specific information your broker or finance team confirms. If finance confirms that some taxes are recoverable, track the cash paid at entry and the estimated recoverable amount separately from the cost used to rank offers.

Your comparison can be as simple as four formulas.

```text
Landed cost = supplier quote total + buyer-paid charges outside the quote
Landed cost per roll = landed cost / rolls received
Gross area = sum of roll count × roll width × roll length for each line
Landed cost per usable m² = landed cost / estimated usable area in m²
```

Keep unpriced items visible next to the total. For example, an estimate with final delivery still open should say which service is missing. This gives your team a specific question for the supplier or forwarder and avoids a detailed subtotal that appears more complete than it is.

## Worked example for a twelve-roll order

The figures below are hypothetical purchasing inputs in US dollars for an equivalent order of twelve rolls. Each roll measures 1.52 m × 15 m. Both shipments are compared at the buyer’s warehouse, including a buyer-paid receiving service. Offer A is FCA at the seller’s named origin point. Offer B is DAP at the buyer’s named warehouse with carriage to that point included. The buyer purchases separate cargo cover in Offer A. The import and broker amounts are illustrative inputs and are held equal here to show the effect of delivery charges.

| Line item | Offer A, FCA | Offer B, DAP |
|---|---:|---:|
| Supplier quote per roll | $300.00 | $344.00 |
| Supplier quote for 12 rolls | $3,600.00 | $4,128.00 |
| Freight from origin to destination terminal | $520.00 | Included |
| Separate buyer cargo insurance | $42.00 | No separate premium in example |
| Brokerage paid by buyer | $90.00 | $90.00 |
| Import charges paid by buyer | $210.00 | $210.00 |
| Local delivery to warehouse | $95.00 | Included |
| Receiving service at warehouse | $75.00 | $75.00 |
| **Total to the receiving point** | **$4,632.00** | **$4,503.00** |
| **Landed cost per roll** | **$386.00** | **$375.25** |
| **Landed cost per gross m²** | **$16.93** | **$16.46** |

Both orders contain 273.60 m² of gross film area. Offer A starts $528 below Offer B on the supplier invoice. After adding the freight, cover, and delivery charges assigned to the buyer, Offer A ends up $129 higher at the agreed receiving point. The totals use the unrounded line amounts. The per-roll and area figures are rounded only for display.

Check what would change the outcome before repeating the order. If Offer A’s freight estimate falls from $520 to $391 and all other figures hold, the two totals match. If Offer B has an additional $130 charge outside its current scope, it moves from $129 cheaper to $1 more expensive. Ask for written confirmation on the charges large enough to change the decision, even when you can calculate a preliminary winner.

## Account for the area the shop can actually use

Compare gross area when the roll widths, film properties, and intended jobs are equivalent. When roll widths or pattern layouts differ, estimate usable area from your own cutting records or trial. A narrow roll can be suitable for certain parts and require a different layout for others. A cost-per-square-meter comparison should be paired with a check that the roll fits the panels or templates you buy it for.

If the shop estimates that 90% of the gross area will be usable for this particular mix of jobs, each illustrated order yields 246.24 m². Offer A then costs approximately $18.81 per usable m² and Offer B approximately $18.29. The 90% figure is a planning assumption. Use the same cutting mix for both offers initially. Where the widths, usable lengths, or approved applications differ, document the reason and calculate each yield separately.

A working sheet can also track rolls that need to be set aside for inspection, sample work, or training if they belong to that opening shipment. Keep those quantities specific to your plan. After receipt, record the actual shipped quantities, invoice additions, and material used. The next comparison can then start with the observed cost instead of relying entirely on the original estimate.

## Make the purchase decision with timing and cash in view

Record the estimated date the shipment will be available at the warehouse, the order’s total payment commitment, and the product approval status. If one option requires a larger minimum, show how many weeks of stock that quantity represents at the shop’s current usage. Also show how you plan to receive the reorder if the first order is smaller. A narrow cost advantage can disappear when the initial order includes film you need to store for longer than expected.

Confirm what the supplier will do if an item is damaged at receipt or if the carton contains a different SKU. Ask which photos, carrier records, label details, and deadlines are needed to report a shortage or damage. Price and delivery belong in the cost worksheet. Stock approval, timing confidence, and claim handling belong next to it so the buyer can see all the information needed to place the order.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is freight included in landed cost?

Yes, when freight is needed to reach the agreed endpoint. Add the parts paid outside the supplier quote. Label the freight already included and verify that the named endpoint matches the receiving location in your comparison.

### Is landed cost per roll useful if suppliers offer different roll sizes?

It helps show what each physical roll costs to receive, but sizes change the amount of film you buy. Add a gross area comparison and check usable area against your normal panels, cutting patterns, and material requirements.

### Should sample shipping be included in the bulk order total?

Track it separately if it was a standalone qualification expense. Show a credit in the bulk worksheet only if the supplier has confirmed that it applies to this order and the quote has yet to reflect it.

### Can I compare one quote that still has unpriced charges?

Compare the verified amounts, show what each subtotal excludes, and run a range for the largest open item. Ask for written confirmation before treating that quote as the lower delivered cost.

### What should I check when a supplier revises the unit price?

Ask for the entire revised quote, then check quantity, currency, validity, and delivery scope as well as the price. If the minimum order, packing, or freight coverage changed, update the relevant landed-cost rows before choosing an offer.
